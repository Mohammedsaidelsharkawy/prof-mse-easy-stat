# Prof MSE Easy Stat

An offline web app that turns an Excel mastersheet into a formatted Word statistical report: methods paragraph, CONSORT flowchart, results tables, auto-written comments and figures.

## Three phases: 🧱 Build · 🎲 Generate · 📊 Analyse

A bar at the top of the app moves between the three phases of a study; each can be used on its own (Analyse works with your own data as before), and one project keeps all three.

### 🎲 Generate the data

Fills the master sheet designed in phase 1 with **simulated patients** — for teaching, for testing the analysis plan before the data exist, and for checking the sample size:

- **Patients and groups**: the number of patients, the groups from the design's randomisation list or a ratio (1:1, 2:1 …), and a seed (the same seed gives the same data).
- **Expected values for each group and visit**: the mean of each number (with its SD — one for the variable, or its own for any group and visit — normal or skewed / log-normal), the % “Yes” of each yes/no item and the % of each category — empty cells use the expected values set in the design (each library variable has sensible defaults, e.g. age 50 ± 12, hypertension 30%). Different values between the groups make a treatment effect; different values over the visits make a change over time; the correlation of a patient's visits is set too.
- **How numbers are made**, for each variable:
  - **Mean & SD**;
  - **Min & Max**: the values spread between them, centred, with a min–max for each group and visit if you wish;
  - **Both**: mean & SD kept within the min–max; a value outside is drawn again.
- **Difference between the groups** (or the change over the visits in one group), for each variable:
  - **As typed**;
  - **Significant**: the group means or percentages are set far enough apart for about 95% power with these patients;
  - **Not significant**: the same values in every group.
  - The values used are shown after → in the cells, and the P value reached in a badge (✓ / ✗). If the P value is on the wrong side, the variable is drawn again, up to 15 times.
  - Checked over 5 seeds: numbers and yes/no with two groups, three groups (ANOVA) and one group over visits all came out as asked.
  - The power calculation ignores this setting and uses the values typed.
- **🎲 Redraw** one variable: only that variable is drawn again, for the same patients and groups, and the others stay as they are (↺ goes back to the first draw).
- **Each variable with its results**: a card per variable with its settings, its cells, what was asked, and its full results (tables, figure and report comment, as in Analyse step 10), updated live. Or **all variables in one table**.
- Missing values and dropouts are **0% by default**.
- **⋯ More** on a variable:
  - **shape**: normal, skewed to the right (log-normal), skewed to the left, or uniform, all with the mean and SD asked;
  - **round to** 0.1, 0.5, 1, 2, 5 or 10 (digit preference, e.g. blood pressure to the nearest 5);
  - **a quick effect**: Cohen's d for numbers or a relative risk for yes/no, which fills the other groups' values (at every visit after the first);
  - **a pattern over the visits** for each group (flat, steady change, fast then plateau, slow then fast, change then back) by a chosen amount;
  - **an effect only in a subgroup** (e.g. only in diabetics), the other patients getting the first group's values.
- **Missing values are**: at random (MCAR), more when another variable is high (MAR), more when the value itself is high (MNAR), or more in one group, to show how complete-case results can mislead.
- **Centre effect**: with sites, an intraclass correlation for the numbers (patients of one site alike).
- **Scenarios**: save the settings under a name (expected, optimistic, pessimistic …) and open them again, or **∅ No differences** (the null scenario: about 5% of tests significant by chance).
- **Several datasets** in one zip, from consecutive seeds (e.g. one per student), with an **answer key**: what was asked, what came out and the P value of each variable in each dataset.
- **Power curve**: the power of the main outcome for seven sample sizes, drawn with the 80% line, giving the number of patients for 80% and 90%.
- **Checks**:
  - each shape matches its mean, SD and skewness, and rounding to 0.5 holds for every value;
  - MNAR lowers the observed mean, MAR misses older patients more often, and "more in one group" works;
  - the subgroup effect appears only in that subgroup;
  - the centre effect reaches the ICC asked (0.095 for 0.1, 0.28 for 0.3, after standardising the site effects);
  - the simulated power for d = 0.5 is 52%, 79% and 94% with 64, 128 and 200 patients, against 50%, 80% and 94% by statsmodels.
- **Links between variables** (a correlation, e.g. age with hypertension), **dates** in their clinical order (enrolment, admission, surgery, discharge, death) with the days between them, **calculated columns** computed (BMI, length of stay, change, totals), and the design's **rules** kept.
- **Realism**: missing values (all variables or each one), patients who drop out (their later visits missing), outliers, and **messy entries** for cleaning practice (units typed in cells, ×10 typing errors, 999, male / MALE / M, duplicated IDs).
- **Results of each variable**, analysed and drawn as in 📊 Analyse step 10 — the summary with each group's data (as the Word file), or the figure with all the tables (descriptive statistics, the tests and the one used in the report, pairwise comparisons and effect sizes), with the report's comment — and above each variable what the generator was **asked** for it (e.g. Technique A 30% Yes · Technique B 30% Yes). An overview lists every variable's test and P value; click one to show it alone. **Live** (on by default): a changed expected value, SD, missing % or link generates the data again with the same seed and redraws the results. The data loaded in Analyse are not touched.
- **Check**: what came out against what was asked, for each variable, group and visit (folded under the results); and the first rows.
- **Power**: the study is simulated many times (e.g. 200) and the main outcome tested each time (Welch's t-test, ANOVA, Chi-square, or a paired t-test for one group): the share of significant results is the power, with its 95% CI.
- **Output**: the simulated master sheet in Excel (as in phase 1, with the formulas' results) or **📊 Analyse these data** in one click. The file is named “Simulated …”, its instructions say so, and it carries a hidden flag: the app then shows a notice, and two marks are offered — a “SIMULATED DATA” watermark in the Word report and a sentence in the statistical analysis — both off unless ticked. Step 10 compares the generator's targets with the data.

Checked on 20,000 simulated patients: means, SDs, percentages, skewed (log-normal) values and the correlation between variables match their targets (e.g. a latent correlation of 0.5 with 30% “Yes” gives a point-biserial r of 0.387 against 0.379 in theory), and the simulated power of a two-group comparison (d = 0.75, 60 per group) is 97%, as by the formula.

Simulated items of one scale answer together.
- **Eligibility checklist**: inclusion and exclusion criteria as yes/no columns and "Eligible" by formula. They are listed in the protocol and left out of the shell tables.
- **Several answers** ("tick all that apply"): one Yes/No column per option. Analyse treats the options as one item group; the CRF prints tick boxes, REDCap a checkbox field and the XLSForm a select_multiple.
- **Expected values with Min and Max**: besides the mean, SD and distribution of a number, a simulated **Min** and **Max**. A value outside is drawn again (a truncated distribution, so values don't pile up at the limits); empty uses the allowed range. **⬆ From an existing sheet** makes a design from a master sheet you already have (Excel or CSV): the ID column (and its format, e.g. K001), the group column and its arms, the visits from the column suffixes (Pain_Baseline, Pain_M3, Pain_M6 → one variable at three visits; a lone _m2 is a unit), each column's type (number, yes/no — also 0/1 —, categories, date, text), units from the names (Age (years), BMI_kg_m2), ranges and categories from the values, the library's sections and ranges when a name is known — and the values become the expected values for the generator. **↶ ↷ Undo / redo** (Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y) for every change of the design.

**🧱 Master sheet builder** (step 1) designs the data collection sheet of a new study before any data exist, in six steps:

1. **Study**: title, design, planned number of patients, the patient ID format (P001 …), conventions (column names such as Age_years, categories typed as words — the default — or as codes with a codebook, missing values as empty cells or 999, the date format) and the layout (one row per patient, one row per visit, or one sheet per visit), with optional **double data entry**. **Sample size** from the main outcome and its expected values: two groups (Student's t-test for a number, Chi-square for yes/no, any allocation ratio) or one group before and after (paired t-test), with the significance level, power and dropout; one click makes it the planned number, and the paragraph is copied for the protocol and put in the methods section of the analysis (checked against statsmodels: d = 0.5 → 64 per group, ratio 2:1 → 48 + 96, paired → 34; 30% vs 15% at 90% power → 161 per group).
2. **Groups & visits**: the study arms, the visits (each gives the column suffix, e.g. Pain_VAS_cm_Month3), and a **randomisation list** — permuted blocks of chosen sizes or simple, stratified by categorical variables, reproducible from its seed — that can fill the Group column in ID order.
3. **Variables**: a library of about 90 ready-made variables (demographics, medical history, vital signs, laboratory tests, scores, operative data, outcomes, diagnostic accuracy) with their units, allowed ranges and categories; sections with colours; for each variable the label, column name (automatic or your own), type (number, yes/no, categories, date, time, text, or an **Excel formula**: BMI, days between two dates, age from the date of birth, change from the first to the last visit, sum or mean of items), unit, decimals, allowed range, categories, the visits where it is collected, item group, role, definition, whether it is required, and **skip logic** (*asked only when* another variable has a category, e.g. Complication type only when Complications = Yes: greyed cells in Excel, “if …” in the CRF, branching logic in REDCap, left empty by the generator). Quick entry: **+ List** (type or paste many variables at once — library names are recognised; `Name (unit)`, `Name: A / B / C`, `@all`, `@last`), **⧉ duplicate**, and **scales with their items** — PHQ-9, GAD-7 or any Likert scale: one column per item and the total by formula (the generator makes the items of a scale answer together).
4. **Rules**: checks between columns (e.g. discharge ≥ admission), highlighted where broken.
5. **Preview**: the sheet as it will look, with a list of problems (duplicate or long column names, numbers without a range, formulas not complete) and of the design: two variables with the same label, a randomised trial with one group, no primary outcome, a visit with no variable, skip logic pointing to a removed variable or category, and a planned number below the sample size.
6. **Export**: the **Excel master sheet** — a coloured section row over the column names, frozen ID column and header, IDs pre-filled, drop-down lists of the categories, allowed ranges with input messages, out-of-range values in red, broken rules in yellow, formula columns in grey, a codebook, instructions, the randomisation list, double entry with a sheet marking every difference, and the study's setup in a hidden sheet; a printable **case report form** in Word (check boxes, blanks with units and ranges, a table per visit); and a **REDCap data dictionary**.

When the filled master sheet is uploaded, its setup is applied by itself: the rows not entered yet are left out, 999 becomes missing, and the groups (in their order), labels with units, categories (and the labels of codes), item groups, roles, definitions and visits (as repeated measures) are set. Starter templates (two-arm trial, cohort, diagnostic study, before–after, surgical outcomes), designs saved in the browser, and design files to share complete it.

## Statistics

- Normality: Shapiro–Wilk, or Kolmogorov–Smirnov with Lilliefors correction
- Between groups: independent t-test, Mann–Whitney U, one-way ANOVA (Tukey), Kruskal–Wallis, Chi-square, Fisher's exact
- Optional: Welch's ANOVA with Games-Howell post hoc for unequal variances, Dunn's post hoc after Kruskal–Wallis, pairwise Chi-square / Fisher post hoc (Bonferroni) for categorical variables, group × time interaction by mixed (split-plot) ANOVA
- Within subjects: paired t-test and Wilcoxon signed-rank (two timepoints); repeated-measures ANOVA (Greenhouse–Geisser) and Friedman test (three or more), with Bonferroni-adjusted post hoc comparisons against the first timepoint
- Correlation (optional): Pearson or Spearman between numeric variables, with scatter plots of significant pairs
- Effect sizes and 95% confidence intervals
- Advanced (optional): multiple linear regression (B, β, 95% CI, R²), binary logistic regression (univariable and multivariable OR, Nagelkerke R²), ROC curves (AUC with DeLong 95% CI, Youden cut-off, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV), ANCOVA (adjusted means and Bonferroni pairwise comparisons), Kaplan-Meier survival with the log-rank test, Cox regression (hazard ratios), McNemar / McNemar-Bowker for paired categorical data, Cohen's kappa, ICC with Bland-Altman analysis, diagnostic accuracy from a 2×2 table (sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, likelihood ratios, exact CIs), Cronbach's alpha
- Binary variables: relative risk or odds ratio
- Stage 4 additions, checked against Python: TOST (statsmodels ttost_ind), Mantel-Haenszel OR, CI and Breslow-Day (statsmodels StratifiedTable), conditional logistic regression (statsmodels ConditionalLogit), Yuen (SciPy trimmed t-test), exact Mann-Whitney (SciPy), Cochran-Armitage (R prop.trend.test), JZS Bayes factor and effect sizes (pingouin), sample size for two means (statsmodels)
- Stage 8 additions, checked against Python: Poisson, ordinal and multinomial models, LMM with covariates (statsmodels), negative binomial coefficients (statsmodels; SEs at fixed α as R glm.nb), Deming slope, PCA eigenvalues, KMO and Bartlett (numpy), partial correlation (pingouin), two-way ANOVA type II (statsmodels), mediation paths (OLS), NRI, IDI and AUC (sklearn)
- Further advanced analyses, checked against Python: cumulative incidence (Aalen-Johansen, by hand; lifelines jitters tied times), restricted cubic spline basis, knots and Wald tests (statsmodels OLS), quantile regression coefficients and robust SEs (statsmodels QuantReg), Fleiss' kappa (statsmodels), repeated-measures correlation (pingouin rm_corr), Firth logistic regression (independent scipy maximisation of the penalized likelihood), Uno's AUC(t) (an independent numpy implementation), and logistic regression covariance (statsmodels).
- Trials and repeated outcomes, checked against Python: Newcombe's CI and the Farrington-Manning score statistic (statsmodels), GEE coefficients, robust SEs and working correlations for binomial, Poisson and Gaussian models with exchangeable, independence and AR(1) structures (statsmodels GEE; AR(1) uses the real visit distance, which equals statsmodels' when no visit is missing), and the random-intercept logistic model (an independent maximisation of the likelihood integrated with scipy).

The original engine's output (1,808 test values) was checked against R 4.6.1. The paired, Wilcoxon signed-rank and correlation tests were checked against SciPy 1.18 on 300 random datasets. Linear and logistic regression were checked against statsmodels 0.15, and ROC results against scikit-learn 1.9, on 200 random datasets. Welch's ANOVA, Games-Howell, mixed ANOVA and ANCOVA were checked against pingouin 0.7, Dunn's test against scikit-posthocs, pairwise Chi-square against SciPy, and Kaplan-Meier / log-rank against lifelines 0.30, on 120 random datasets. Median survival follows SPSS: the first time survival falls to 50% or below. Lilliefors, McNemar(-Bowker), Cohen's kappa, odds ratios and Clopper-Pearson CIs were checked against statsmodels, ICC and Cronbach's alpha against pingouin, and Cox regression (Efron ties) against lifelines, on 150 random datasets. Weighted kappa matches statsmodels, Kendall's tau-b SciPy, ICC(C,1) / ICC(C,k) pingouin, Kaplan-Meier 95% CIs lifelines, Durbin-Watson statsmodels, Huynh-Feldt epsilon pingouin, Holm statsmodels, and skewness / kurtosis SciPy. The ROC cut-offs by sensitivity / specificity target, the DeLong comparison of two AUCs and the Clopper-Pearson CIs were checked against scikit-learn, statsmodels and a reference DeLong implementation on 200 random datasets.

- **Combined figures**: the panel letters (A, B, C …) now sit in a band of their own above each panel, with space between the panels, instead of covering the axis numbers (also for “figures as panels” in Word).
- **Long legends**: a legend above or below a chart that is wider than the chart now wraps onto a second line instead of being cut at both edges (single charts, panels, PNG and SVG). The default figures are unchanged.
- **An arm of the design with no patients yet**: a master sheet from phase 1 keeps the planned arm (shown with “—”) instead of becoming a one-group report.

**🧱 Build**
- **🔐 Digital allocation tool (.html)**: one offline page with the randomisation list encrypted by a password (AES-GCM). The next allocation appears only after the patient ID, the person's initials and the ticks for eligibility and signed consent; an ID cannot be allocated twice; each allocation is logged with the date and time and a chained SHA-256 check, so a changed log is detected (**Verify the log**); the log exports as CSV. Strata and sites have their own lists. Checked: wrong password refused, allocations as on the list, an edited entry caught.
- **Screening log** (Excel option): every patient screened, each inclusion and exclusion criterion, Eligible worked out by formula, consented, randomised and the reason when not, with the counts at the top. Uploaded to Analyse, its counts fill the flowchart (assessed, not eligible, declined, other reasons).
- **Data queries** (Excel option): a Queries sheet — patient (drop-down of the IDs), column, value, question, answer, open / answered / closed, open ones in yellow and the counts at the top.
- **🔎 Source data verification plan (.xlsx)**: a random sample of the patients (the % chosen, by site), and a checklist for each: consent, every eligibility criterion, the allocation, each key outcome at each visit, adverse events and withdrawals.
- **Normal ranges by sex and age**: rules on any number (e.g. haemoglobin 13.5–17.5 for men and 12–15.5 for women from 18 years; ♂/♀ fills the usual adult values for haemoglobin, haematocrit, creatinine, ferritin and uric acid). Excel shades each value against its own rule, and Analyse reports the % outside the sex- and age-specific ranges (matches pandas: 33% and 41%).
- **💰 Study budget (.xlsx)**: cost per visit, per test (the investigations of the design) and per patient, fixed costs, times the patients to enrol, with the totals by year from the recruitment rate — all Excel formulas (checked by LibreOffice).
- **QR codes on labels**: ⬇ QR codes / QR per visit (ID and visit), for phone scanning. Every code tested decodes with zxing (short IDs, Unicode, 120 characters).
- **🔍 Design review**: at the top of the Export tab: primary outcome, sample size, outcome definitions, ranges, units, skip logic, visits used, eligibility, allocation concealment and a required primary outcome, each ✓ or ⚠ with what to do.

**🎲 Generate**
- **SD for each group and visit**: a ± box beside every mean. Empty, it uses the variable's SD. Checked on 3,000 patients: 5.02 and 14.87 for 5 and 15.
- **Exact match**: each group's mean, SD and % come out exactly as typed (numbers rescaled, categories given out by largest remainders, the mean corrected after rounding), for tidy worked examples. The power simulation still draws freely. With 20 patients per group: means 40.00 and 50.00, 30% and 45% exactly; SDs within the rounding of whole years.
- **Counts**: Poisson (mean 2.03, SD 1.44 for 2 and √2) and negative binomial with the SD asked (SD 3.10 for 3), always whole numbers.
- **Ordinal outcomes**: the other groups' categories from the first group's by a proportional odds ratio. An ordinal logistic regression (statsmodels) recovered 2.46 for 2.5.
- **Measurements that go together**: laboratory panels and vital signs linked when both are in the design: urea with creatinine (r 0.69 for 0.7), ALT with AST (0.79 for 0.8), haemoglobin with haematocrit (0.90), systolic with diastolic, height with weight, glucose with HbA1c and others.
- **Operator effect**: patients of the same surgeon or anaesthetist (any column) alike, on every number or one. A mixed model (statsmodels) gave an ICC of 0.21 for 0.2.
- **Harder survival**: hazards that change after half the follow-up (crossing curves: Cox HR 0.47 then 2.0 for 0.5 then 2), and a **competing event** (e.g. death from other causes) that ends the follow-up.
- **Matched case-control pairs**: a control for each case with the same matching categories and numbers within ± a tolerance, a pair number, and the exposure from an odds ratio. Conditional logistic regression recovered 3.4 for 3 (1,000 pairs).
- **From a published table**: paste a paper's Table 1 (mean (SD), mean ± SD, median [IQR], n (%), a category per row), choose where the Total column is, and the rows are matched to the design's variables; exact match is switched on. The data stay marked as simulated.
- **⬇ Practice exercises (.docx)**: questions on the dataset (patients and duplicates, each comparison with its test, missing values, the planted errors, survival), and the model answers worked out from the data on a second page.

**🗂 Tables of the report** (⚙ Options of a table)
- **Pairwise P columns** (3+ groups): A vs B, A vs C, B vs C as columns — the table's post hoc test for numbers, Chi-square / Fisher (Bonferroni) for categories. Checked against scipy (Sex, Control vs Dexmedetomidine: 0.763).
- **Groups in this table**: tick the groups to show (e.g. the two active arms without control); the table is analysed again with only them, and the footnote says so. Also the **order** of the groups for that table.
- **Sub-headings inside a table**: a heading row above any variable (e.g. Vital signs, Laboratory) without splitting the table.
- **Risk difference and NNT** (two groups, yes/no rows): RD in percentage points with the Newcombe CI (matches statsmodels: −10.9 to 39.9) and the number needed to treat; when the CI includes 0 it runs through ∞ (Altman), the negative end being the other direction.
- **Style per table**: APA, NEJM, Lancet or compact for one table only, in the preview and in Word.
- **Edit in the preview**: click a row name to rename it, drag ⠿ to move it, × to leave it out.
- **Highlight rules**: significant rows in bold, rows with more than 20% missing in grey, large effects (d ≥ 0.8, RR ≥ 2 or ≤ 0.5) highlighted, each explained in the footnote when used.

**📊 Report**
- **🌲 Subgroup forest plot**: the primary outcome's mean difference (Welch CI) or relative risk within each category of the chosen subgroups, square sizes by weight, and the **P for interaction** (Cochran's Q across the subgroups). Matches scipy to the third decimal (e.g. Sex: −0.24 and 0.90, P interaction 0.115). Can be added at the end of the results.
- **🧐 Statistical review (SAMPL)**: 14 items of the SAMPL guidelines (primary outcome, sample size, tests named, assumptions, summaries, effect sizes with CIs, exact P values, α, multiple comparisons, missing data, paired data, software, covariates, subgroups), each ✓ or ⚠ with what to do.
- **⚖ Sensitivity analyses**: the primary outcome as analysed, per protocol (when set), without outliers (Tukey fences), with missing values imputed, and adjusted for the baseline variables (linear or logistic regression) in one table. The adjusted estimate matches statsmodels OLS (0.375, −0.256 to 1.007, P 0.241).
- **📐 Planned vs actual**: patients, dropout, SD and difference of the primary outcome, planned against observed, and the power for the planned difference with the patients analysed. The plan comes with a master sheet made in phase 1, or is typed in.
- **✍ Discussion draft**: main finding, against what was expected, strengths and limitations from the design and the data (design, sample size reached, missing data, centres, small sample, unadjusted comparisons), and a conclusion, with [brackets] for what only you can write.
- **⬇ Interactive results page (.html)**: one file to send to a supervisor: search, significant only, open / close each table, every figure.
- **🕘 Report versions**: every Word export is kept in this browser with its text; download any of them again, or compare two (words added and removed, P values that changed).
- **Journal figure rules** in Export figures: Elsevier, Springer Nature and PLOS presets (format, dpi, width, text size), and more widths (8.4, 9, 13.2, 14, 19, 19.05 cm).
- **🔎 Text against the tables**: every decimal or % written in a table's comment is looked for in that table; the ones not found are listed with their sentence (no false alarm on three example reports; a planted 99.9 was caught).

**🎲 Generate**
- **Time to event**: exponential times with a hazard ratio, the % with the event, the follow-up and early censoring, filling the event, the time and the date of the event. Over six seeds the hazard ratio averaged 0.61 for 0.6 asked.
- **Risk factors**: a yes/no outcome from predictors with odds ratios (per unit for numbers), each group keeping its own %. Logistic regression recovered OR 1.51 and 3.26 for 1.5 and 3.
- **Regression**: a number built from its predictors (b per unit). OLS recovered 0.297 and 5.0 for 0.3 and 5.
- **Confounding**: one group gets more of a baseline variable (odds ratio), so crude and adjusted results differ.
- **More shapes**: two subpopulations (bimodal), many zeros (zero-inflated, % chosen), piling at the maximum or minimum (ceiling / floor).
- **Patient slopes** over the visits, and **Cronbach's α** of a scale's items (within ±0.02 from 0.6 to 0.95).
- **Diagnostic accuracy**: prevalence, sensitivity, specificity and the AUC of a numeric index (25.5 / 79.9 / 89.5% and AUC 0.849 for 25 / 80 / 90% and 0.85).
- **Agreement**: two readers with a kappa (0.605 for 0.6); two methods with a bias and an SD of the differences (Bland-Altman).
- **Recruitment**: steady, slow start, fast start or seasonal, and the patients per site by weights.
- **Protocol deviations and the flowchart**: % excluded at screening (with reasons), lost (later visits empty, Withdrawn and a reason), not adherent, crossed over. The counts go to the flowchart in Analyse with the file.
- **Errors to plant** (messy mode): choose units in cells, × 10 typing, 999, decimal commas, mixed spelling, spelling mistakes, extra spaces, impossible dates and duplicated rows. **⬇ Error key** lists each one (ID, column, correct value, wrong value).

- **Free text**: plausible words for complication types, reasons for withdrawal, occupations and comments.
- **Power by more tests**: Mann-Whitney / Kruskal-Wallis, change from the first visit, ANCOVA adjusted for the first visit, and log-rank.
- **Several outcomes at once**: the chance that all or any are significant, with Holm or Bonferroni.
- **Interim analyses** with O'Brien-Fleming boundaries (2–4 looks): power, early stopping and expected size, and the type I error under no difference (3% in the check).
